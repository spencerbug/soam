## Answers and limits

1. **Can new object-specific information survive complete transient reset?** Yes.
   All three writing mechanisms have much lower single-object error than NOWRITE.
   KV meets the entire acquisition/retention conjunction, including the separate
   one-shot condition. The other writers' conjunction failures do not erase their
   successful isolated-object acquisition.
2. **Does it survive learning similar objects?** KV does on the tested eight-object
   sequences: 100% sampled old-object accuracy and zero measured error change.
   REG retains 87.10% accuracy but exceeds the .02 error-change margin; FAST falls
   to 5.98% with much larger interference. Adjusted lower bounds on both error
   increases exceed the guard. No global retention or capacity guarantee follows.
3. **Does the tested fast recurrent operator beat conventional memory?** No.
   Both adjusted practical-benefit comparisons are precise negatives. FAST and
   REG have equal numerical persistent bytes; KV uses more preallocated storage.
   Its delta write/readout is exactly a normalized online-gradient/direct-map
   computation, so no uniquely recurrent or attractor explanation is identifiable.
4. **What is the cheapest reliable tested choice?** KV is the only mechanism passing
   all frozen reliability criteria. Recommend its explicit memory plus shared
   learned action core for this task, while reporting its 7448 allocated numerical
   bytes against 4120 for REG/FAST and its measured latency tradeoffs. This is a
   constrained choice among tested implementations, not global cost optimality.
5. **What remains untested?** D-H2 reuse versus independently refitting whole models;
   D-H3 selective writing; D3 corrupt/changed relationships; D4 capacity stress;
   learned features/keys, uncertain correspondence, generic geometry, dense recurrent
   plasticity, nonlinear attractor retrieval, consolidation and learned organization.

The experiment deliberately supplies a reversible action family, fixed sensory
projection, full acquisition snapshots, stable appearance correspondence, and a
proprioceptive reference. Random appearance does not encode the independently
sampled relationship, but it makes content-based object addressing easy. The tested
motion core is almost exact. Success is scoped to retaining noisy associations and
using that learned transform after reset, not discovery of arbitrary sensorimotor laws.

False familiarity (0% KV, 32.1% REG, 90.5% FAST in the sample) is a diagnostic from
known-object development thresholds. Scores are not calibrated probabilities;
neither these results nor B2/C make a reliable write gate. The old shared core is
unchanged, so its preserved prediction is not evidence that every memory function
survived: the sequential query guards supply that separate test.

## One recommended follow-up, not activated

A small **full-versus-partial-cue D2 diagnostic** on fresh streams would compare the
same writers at identical acquisition information and memory budgets. It can separate
partial-cue conditioning from damage to the stored key/relationship map, especially
for REG. Freeze paired cue conditions and independent-root margins before execution;
do not reuse these confirmation outcomes as unseen data. This is one proposed fork,
not a new selected experiment or permission for a writer search. D3/D4 remain deferred.
