# SOAM Study C — Hypothesis Preservation Under Ambiguity, with Conditional Tests of Selective Recurrent Computation

## Role and research objective

Act as the principal research scientist and implementation engineer for SOAM Study C.

The overarching research question is:

**Can a recurrent system preserve, track, and selectively attend to multiple competing or complementary hypotheses more accurately or efficiently than competent simpler estimators, without prematurely collapsing to a wrong interpretation?**

The proposed functional modes are:

1. Primed.
2. Tracking/calibrating.
3. Attended.

These are hypotheses about information processing and resource allocation, not mandatory discrete neural states.

**Select and execute only the first bounded experiment: whether explicit candidate states improve inference under genuine ambiguity.**

Selective computation, complementary-object maintenance, independent recurrent banks, and learned controllers are conditional follow-ups. Define their possible research gates, but do not implement or run them automatically.

Do not begin by implementing a complex controller, multi-column architecture, learned attention network, persistent plasticity, or object-memory integration.

## 2. Scope and research questions

Keep three claims separate.

### C-H1 — Explicit hypothesis maintenance

Maintaining separate candidate states may improve calibrated uncertainty, delayed disambiguation, and recovery relative to a competent single-state estimator.

Any benefit may come from representation capacity or explicit state factorization rather than neural gating.

### C-H2 — Selective computation

Given an established multi-state representation, selectively allocating prediction and correction may improve the accuracy–cost tradeoff relative to competent uniform schedules.

This requires a separate experiment.

### C-H3 — Learned allocation

A learned scheduling policy may improve on fixed or uncertainty-based heuristics at an equal resource budget.

This requires a diagnosed limitation of simpler scheduling and a separately selected experiment.

A conventional filter mixture, a single recurrent state, or a fixed multi-state network may suffice. Such outcomes are scientifically useful.

**The first experiment addresses C-H1 only.**

## 3. First experiment: two competing explanations

Build one small synthetic task family with exactly two candidate explanations.

Prefer a construction compatible with the existing prediction/correction infrastructure and competent low-dimensional filter references.

Examples include:

- Two motion regimes that produce identical observations under initial actions but diverge under a diagnostic action.
- Two candidate reference frames that remain observationally equivalent until a revealing movement.
- Two local structures with identical initial sensory histories and different later observations.

Choose one construction.

### Required observability analysis

Demonstrate analytically or numerically that:

1. The initial permitted observation–action history is compatible with both candidates.
2. No estimator receiving that history can reliably identify the eventual true candidate.
3. A later specified observation or action provides discriminating information in designated conditions.
4. Other conditions remain ambiguous throughout the episode.

Verify equivalence for the full permitted input stream, including masks, timestamps, action sequences, focus signals, and metadata.

Prevent incidental information leaks through candidate ordering, random seeds, sequence lengths, initialization, or task identifiers.

Use prescribed action sequences for this first study. Compare informative and uninformative actions without introducing learned action selection.

Include a delayed-disambiguation condition with a sensing gap or intervening motion so that maintaining candidate trajectories matters.

Do not reward arbitrary early commitment to the eventual true label.

## 4. Candidate knowledge, initialization, and information parity

Define exactly what is supplied and what is learned:

- Candidate identities or descriptions.
- Candidate transition and observation models.
- Initial state distributions.
- Prior hypothesis weights.
- Association information.
- Any training labels or calibration targets.

Give all models equivalent access to permitted candidate knowledge and observations.

Physical-state ground truth is evaluator-only. Any hypothesis-label supervision must be explicitly declared, restricted to training/development as appropriate, and accounted for consistently across comparisons.

Candidate states must be initialized from permitted evidence and disclosed priors. Never initialize them from the true latent trajectory.

For shared recurrent candidates, explain how the two slots acquire distinct candidate meanings. Identical states receiving identical inputs through identical dynamics cannot become distinct hypotheses merely because they have different array indices.

Any supplied candidate embeddings, conditional inputs, or candidate-specific initializers are scaffolding and must be counted.

Known-model or true-dynamics estimators may be included as labeled information bounds, not equal-information baselines.

### R3 — Two recurrent states with shared parameters

Maintain two separate transient states using shared learned prediction/correction functions.

Each state represents one candidate explanation.

Maintain explicit candidate support, with evidence evaluated before correction.

This is shared-parameter, separate-state inference. It is not evidence of shared neurons, concurrent attractors in one activity vector, or biological operating modes.

## 6. Evidence scoring and probabilistic evaluation

Use a proper probabilistic score, such as Brier score or negative log likelihood, as the primary endpoint.

Before confirmation:

- Select one primary score.
- Define the episode-level aggregation and phase weighting.
- Specify how every required model produces normalized candidate probabilities.
- Freeze any development-only calibration.
- Define numerical treatment of near-zero probabilities.

Normalization alone does not establish calibration, and an arbitrary normalized evidence score is not automatically a Bayesian posterior.

For conventional mixtures, specify the observation likelihood or calibrated scoring rule. If robustness uses the current observation to modify uncertainty, account for that dependence rather than asserting an unjustified likelihood.

Evaluate each grounded observation once in the belief update. Multiple candidates evaluating the same observation does not create multiple independent observations.

Distinguish:

- Uncertainty before discriminating evidence.
- Resolution after discriminating evidence.
- Candidate-conditioned prediction quality.
- Mixture prediction quality.

Do not rely on mean squared prediction error alone: averaging incompatible predictions can obscure whether alternatives were preserved.

## 7. Primary conditions and bounded robustness checks

Include:

1. **Identical initial evidence, later disambiguation.**
2. **Persistent ambiguity:** no identifying evidence arrives.
3. **Uninformative movement:** action does not separate candidates.
4. **Diagnostic movement:** action makes candidate predictions distinguishable.
5. **Misleading early evidence:** later evidence supports recovery.
6. **Delayed evidence with intervening motion or occlusion.**

Add a bounded secondary robustness block distinguishing:

- Sensor corruption.
- A genuine change between represented regimes.
- Evidence supporting the alternative candidate.
- Evidence inconsistent with both known candidates.

Define whether genuine changes are possible in the model and what transition assumptions are supplied.

Do not expect a model assigning exactly zero probability to change to recover without an explicit recovery mechanism.

Unknown-candidate rejection is a separate decision from choosing between the two known candidates. A normalized two-way distribution cannot by itself establish that either candidate is adequate.

Do not introduce oracle corruption flags except in separately labeled controls.

When corruption and genuine change generate identical permitted histories, require appropriate uncertainty and report the information limit.

## 9. First-experiment research gate

Distinguish:

### Supported explicit-state benefit

Explicit candidate states provide a practically meaningful improvement over competent single-state controls under the frozen resource and accuracy requirements.

### Capacity explanation

The larger single-state control accounts for the apparent advantage.

### Conventional mixture sufficient

The conventional mixture matches or exceeds the recurrent alternative at the relevant resource tradeoff.

### Single-state inference sufficient

A competent single-state model maintains useful uncertainty and resolves the task without a meaningful explicit-state advantage.

### Inconclusive or invalid

Precision is insufficient, controls are incompetent, observability is incorrect, or execution fails.

Do not equate a wide interval with equivalence or an implementation failure with scientific falsification.

No outcome establishes a need for primed/tracking/attended modes.

Conclude with the simplest supported inference architecture for the tested task.

## 10. Conditional follow-up: complementary states and selective computation

Describe this follow-up in the decision report, but do not implement or execute it automatically.

Use two simultaneously present components whose states can change independently. Both can be valid at once, so their existence must not be forced into a single mutually exclusive probability distribution.

Hold the selected multi-state architecture fixed and compare:

- Full prediction and correction.
- Freezing unattended states.
- Motion-only tracking of unattended states.
- Uniform or round-robin allocation under a fixed budget.
- A simple uncertainty- or innovation-based allocation rule.

Make observation availability and computational allocation separate experimental variables.

Label supplied focus schedules and association information. Keep permitted observations matched where testing computation alone.

**Beating frozen states is insufficient to establish useful selective computation.** Require improvement over competent scheduling controls on a declared accuracy–cost frontier.

Count deferred work and catch-up costs. Never silently reset an unattended state from ground truth.

Test continuity after attention switches, stale-state error, interference, long-horizon drift, and worst-case computation.

Define operating modes through independent controls before imposing categorical labels:

```text
sensory_priority[i]
motion_update_priority[i]
inference_compute_budget[i]
attention_priority[i]
```

A learned controller remains conditional on a specific, measured limitation of the fixed policies.
